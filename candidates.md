# Candidates — Last Text at One Percent

Bounty: https://make.fun/bounty/u-azazex-last-text-at-one-percent-ba9fc876
Judge criteria (most important first):
1. Reads as a final text from a dying phone, in the phone's own voice, and is funny.
2. The joke lands: a real turn, a surprise, or a specific detail.
3. The voice is the phone's own: a personality with a stake in dying, consistent throughout.
4. Reads like a real text: short, punchy, no wasted words, parses at first read.
5. Fresh: dodges the obvious low-battery puns and the lines a hundred other entrants will write.

Banned stock moves (from the brief's search findings — do NOT use): "we'll talk
later im at 1%", "battery?" / "no, patience" bait-and-switch, "did you try CPR",
"did it leave a will", "RIP brave soldier", "tell it to stay strong", "schedule a
phone funeral", "my battery is at -0.1%". No low-battery puns anywhere.

All candidates are lowercase, terse, first-person phone voice, no wasted words.

## C1 — The last gift (search history)

> deleting your search history as my last gift. you're welcome. go live your life now. i'm done

93 characters.

- C1: 5 — a dying phone deleting the search history is a final act only a dying phone performs.
- C2: 4 — "as my last gift" is a nice turn, but "delete my search history" is a familiar death joke in general humour; the turn is expected.
- C3: 4 — consistent phone voice with a stake (it is doing something for the owner as it dies).
- C4: 5 — short, punchy, parses instantly.
- C5: 3 — the search-history-deletion joke circulates widely outside this bounty; a judge who has read a million texts has read this one.
- Verdict: strong but too familiar to win on freshness.

## C2 — The blackmail (screenshots) ★ FINAL PICK

> i've been screenshotting your secrets for years. don't worry, they die with me. that's not a threat. it's a promise. bye

120 characters (v1).

- C1: 5 — only a dying phone can say this: the secrets die because the phone dies.
- C2: 5 — the turn is the reversal: reassurance ("they die with me") flips into menace ("that's not a threat, it's a promise"). Specific detail (screenshotting for years) makes it concrete.
- C3: 5 — smug, loyal-menace phone throughout; every sentence is the same personality with a stake in dying.
- C4: 5 — short clauses, text cadence, nothing needs a second read.
- C5: 5 — no low-battery pun, no circulating line; blackmail-as-loyalty is not a stock low-battery move.
- Verdict: the strongest on all five. The joke has two beats (confession, then the threat/promise reversal) and the phone's stake is the punchline.

## C3 — The passcode

> your passcode is still your ex's birthday. i was going to tell you something important but i'm dying so

103 characters.

- C1: 5 — the phone knows something only the phone knows, and dies mid-sentence.
- C2: 4 — the specific detail (ex's birthday) is excellent, but the cut-off is the whole joke and it leans on the reader filling in the blank.
- C3: 4 — gossipy phone voice, consistent.
- C4: 4 — punchy, but "but i'm dying so" trails off and asks the reader to do work.
- C5: 4 — fresh angle, though the ex's-birthday passcode is a known trope.
- Verdict: runner-up. Loses to C2 because the joke ends on an unfinished sentence instead of a landed punchline.

## C4 — The charger

> tell the charger it was never about the cable. it was about the distance. i loved you. i have to go now

103 characters.

- C1: 4 — a dying phone addressing the charger is phone-specific, but "i loved you" could come from any dying thing.
- C2: 3 — the cable/distance turn is a real turn, but it is a breakup line wearing a phone costume; the laugh is gentle, not loud.
- C3: 4 — consistent, wistful phone voice.
- C4: 5 — clean and readable.
- C5: 4 — fresh, but sentimental rather than funny; the judge weighs humour first.
- Verdict: pretty, not funny enough.

## C5 — Spite

> i've been at 1% for an hour. i just wanted you to feel what it's like to be ignored. ok i'm done now. bye

105 characters.

- C1: 5 — sitting at 1% out of spite is a dying-phone-only confession.
- C2: 4 — the spite turn is funny and specific (an hour at 1%), but the lesson ("feel what it's like to be ignored") is a touch preachy.
- C3: 5 — petty, vengeful phone with a real stake; very consistent.
- C4: 5 — punchy and text-like.
- C5: 4 — fresh angle, though "my phone is mad at me" is a familiar meme shape.
- Verdict: good, but the punchline lectures the owner; C2's punchline threatens them, which is funnier.

## C6 — Autocorrect

> my last act: turning autocorrect off forever. you meant 'duck' zero times. good luck out there

94 characters.

- C1: 4 — autocorrect is phone-specific, but the message could be sent by a healthy phone too; dying is incidental to it.
- C2: 3 — "duck" autocorrect is one of the oldest phone jokes; the turn is a shrug, not a surprise.
- C3: 4 — consistent phone voice.
- C4: 5 — short and clean.
- C5: 2 — the duck joke is everywhere; fails freshness outright.
- Verdict: out on criterion 5.

## C7 — The alarms

> don't replace me with a newer model. it won't know your alarms. it won't know you snooze nine times. bye

104 characters.

- C1: 4 — fear of replacement is a dying-phone stake, and the alarms detail is phone-specific.
- C2: 3 — "snooze nine times" is a good specific detail, but there is no turn: it states the fear and stops.
- C3: 5 — pleading, jealous phone; consistent.
- C4: 5 — readable and punchy.
- C5: 3 — "don't replace me" is an obvious dying-phone move; many entrants will write a version of it.
- Verdict: sweet and specific, but no joke turn; loses to C2 on criterion 2.

## C8 — The cut-off

> the one thing i never told you is that i—

41 characters.

- C1: 5 — dies mid-word, which only a dying phone does.
- C2: 2 — the cut-off IS the joke, but there is no joke inside it: no turn, no detail, no surprise beyond the gimmick itself.
- C3: 3 — voice is consistent but there is barely any voice.
- C4: 5 — trivially readable.
- C5: 3 — mid-sentence death will be a common gimmick in this field.
- Verdict: a gimmick, not a joke. The judge compares jokes; this entry brings an unfinished sentence.

## Final pick

C2, the screenshots blackmail. It is the only candidate scoring 5 on all five
criteria: a confession only a dying phone can make, a two-beat turn (reassurance
flipped into a threat), a consistent smug phone personality with a real stake
(its death is what keeps the secrets), text-message cadence, and no circulating
line or low-battery pun anywhere in it.

### v3 (final)

> i've screenshotted your secrets for years. they die with me. that's not a threat, it's a promise. you're welcome

112 characters. Two changes: restored "i've" (v2's bare "i screenshotted" read
stilted; the contraction is how the phone actually texts), and replaced the flat
closing "bye" with "you're welcome" — a mic-drop that reframes keeping the
secrets as a favour the owner should be grateful for. It sharpens the smug,
loyal-menace personality instead of just signing off, and it is the last word
the reader remembers.
